TECH SUPPORT SCAM • THREAT ALERT

Your Computer Has a Virus!

Or does it?

Fake technical-support scams use frightening pop-ups, phone calls and messages to convince you that your device is infected. Their real goal may be to make you install remote-access software, reveal sensitive information or pay for unnecessary services.

💻 See the Fake Alert

🚨 This Warning Looks Scary

But clicking the number or following the instructions could make things worse.

⚠ suspicious-security-alert.example
🚨

CRITICAL SECURITY WARNING

Your computer may be infected!

Do not close this window.
Call technical support immediately.

📞 1-800-FAKE-HELP

🛡️ Good Choice!

You should NOT call the number displayed in a suspicious pop-up. Close the page or browser and use trusted security software or official support channels instead.

🎭 How the Scam Works

The scam usually follows a simple psychological pattern.

1

Scare You

A pop-up, call or message claims something is seriously wrong.

2

Create Urgency

You're told to act immediately before something terrible happens.

3

Gain Access

The scammer asks you to install software or provide remote access.

4

Demand Money

You may then be pressured to pay for fake repairs or services.

🚨 Warning Signs

If you notice several of these signs, stop and verify.

📞

Unexpected Call

Someone suddenly calls claiming to be technical support.

🚨

Frightening Pop-Up

The screen uses alarming language and tells you to call a number.

⏰

Immediate Action

You're told you must act immediately to prevent account or device damage.

💻

Remote Access

Someone asks you to install software so they can control your computer.

💳

Payment Request

You are asked to pay unexpectedly to "fix" the problem.

🔐

Sensitive Information

They request passwords, card details or other sensitive information.

🎯 What Are They Trying to Get?

The technical problem may be fake. The request is the real danger.

💻

Remote Computer Access

They may ask you to install remote-access software so they can control your device.

🔑

Passwords

They may ask for passwords while pretending to "verify" your account.

💳

Card or Banking Details

A fake repair fee may be used to obtain your payment information.

🔢

Verification Codes

Never share an OTP or security code with an unexpected caller.

🛡️ What Should You Do?

If you receive a suspicious technical-support call, message or pop-up:

  • 🛑 Don't call a number shown in a suspicious pop-up.
  • 🛑 Don't install unknown remote-access software.
  • 🛑 Don't give strangers control of your computer.
  • 🛑 Don't share passwords, OTPs or payment information.
  • 🔍 Close the suspicious page or application.
  • 🌐 Visit the company's official website yourself.
  • 🔐 Use trusted security software to check your device.
  • 📢 Report suspicious activity when appropriate.

🔐 Remember

Real security protection should not depend on panic, pressure or a stranger controlling your device.

Stop. Close. Verify.
