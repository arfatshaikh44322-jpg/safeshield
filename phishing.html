Don't Take the Bait.

A phishing message can look like it came from your bank, delivery company, university, employer or even someone you know. The goal is simple: make you trust the message long enough to give away something valuable.

🔍 Investigate the Scam

🎣 How Phishing Works

The scam usually follows a simple psychological pattern.

1️⃣ The Bait

You receive an unexpected email, SMS, social-media message or notification. The message is designed to look familiar and trustworthy.

2️⃣ The Pressure

The scammer creates an emotional reaction. Maybe your account is supposedly blocked, you've won a prize, your package is stuck, or something requires immediate attention.

3️⃣ The Trap

You are directed to a fake website, asked to download something, or encouraged to reveal information.

4️⃣ The Theft

The information may then be used to access accounts, steal money, impersonate you, or commit further fraud.

🚨 Phishing Red Flags

One warning sign may be accidental. Several together deserve serious attention.

⏰
Unexpected Urgency

"Act now" or "within 10 minutes."

🔗
Suspicious Link

The website address looks unusual or unfamiliar.

🎭
Fake Identity

Someone pretends to represent a trusted organization.

🔑
Sensitive Information

The message asks for passwords, card details or codes.

💰
Payment Request

You are unexpectedly asked to transfer money.

🤫
Secret Instructions

You're told not to tell anyone or verify the request.

🛡️ Your Anti-Phishing Strategy

Don't fight the scammer on their terms.

🛑 STOP

Don't click the link or reply immediately. Give yourself a moment to think.

🔍 CHECK

Look carefully at the sender, website address, message wording and request.

📱 VERIFY

Open the organization's official app or type its website address yourself. Don't use the contact information provided in the suspicious message.

🚫 DON'T SHARE

Never share passwords, OTPs, PINs or other security credentials just because someone asks for them.
