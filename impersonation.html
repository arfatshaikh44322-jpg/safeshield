IMPERSONATION SCAM • STAY ALERT

Is That Really Who They Say They Are?

An impersonation scam happens when someone pretends to be a person, company or organization you trust in order to trick you into sharing information, sending money or taking an action.

The identity may look real. The request is what you need to question.

🔍 Investigate the Scam

📱 Can You Spot the Fake?

Look carefully at this example before revealing the warning signs.

Someone claims to be your friend.

You receive a message from an account using your friend's name and profile picture.

👤
Rahul
✓ Friend • Online
Rahul:

"Hey! I'm in a bit of trouble right now. I lost my phone and I'm using this account.

Can you send me ₹5,000 urgently? I'll return it tonight."

Please don't call my old number. I can't answer it.

🔎 Inspect Message
🚨 Warning signs detected!

The person creates urgency, gives an unusual reason for avoiding verification, and requests money.

A real friend can be verified through another trusted communication method.

🎭 Who Can Scammers Pretend To Be?

Family or Friend

Someone pretends to be someone you know.

They may claim their phone is lost, they're travelling, or they're facing an emergency.

Protection: Call the person using a number you already know.

Bank Employee

A scammer pretends to represent your bank.

They may claim there is suspicious activity on your account and ask for personal information or security codes.

Protection: Contact your bank using its official app or verified contact details.

Police or Government Official

Someone claims to be an authority figure.

They may threaten you with arrest, penalties or legal action unless you immediately pay or provide information.

Protection: Don't panic. Independently verify the claim through official channels.

Boss or Manager

A scammer pretends to be someone from work.

They may ask you to urgently purchase gift cards, transfer money or share confidential information.

Delivery Company

A scammer pretends to be a courier service.

🚨 Warning Signs

Scammers often use the same psychological tricks.

⏰

Extreme Urgency

"You must do this right now."

📞

Avoiding Verification

They give excuses for why you cannot contact the real person.

💰

Money Request

They suddenly ask for a transfer or payment.

🔐

Sensitive Information

They ask for passwords, OTPs or financial information.

🤫

Keep It Secret

They tell you not to contact anyone else about the request.

🔗

Suspicious Link

They want you to use a link they provided instead of an official source.

🧠 What Would You Do?

Someone claiming to be your friend sends you an urgent request for ₹10,000.
They say their phone was stolen and they cannot receive calls.

🛡️ The Verify Before You Trust Rule

Never trust an unexpected request simply because the person appears familiar.

1

STOP

Don't react immediately.

2

CHECK

Look for unusual behavior.

3

VERIFY

Contact them independently.

4

ACT

Only continue when you're sure.

😨 What If You Already Responded?

Don't panic. Take these steps.

1

Stop Contact

Stop replying to the suspicious account or caller.

2

Verify the Real Person

Contact the person or organization through a trusted method you already know.

3

Secure Your Accounts

If you shared credentials or sensitive information, change affected passwords and review account security.

4

Contact Your Bank If Necessary

If money or financial information was involved, contact your financial institution through an official channel as soon as possible.

5

Report the Account

Report suspicious profiles, messages or activity to the relevant platform or organization.

🔐 Familiar Doesn't Always Mean Safe

A profile picture can be copied.
A name can be copied.
A company logo can be copied.

Always verify the person behind the request.
